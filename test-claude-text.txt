Reality check: status of the field (why this is hard)

There are many commercial "AI content detectors" but they disagree a lot and have imperfect accuracy — detectors like GPTZero, QuillBot, Winston AI, PangramLabs, Sapling, etc., are commonly used today. Detection is an active arms race.

Major model vendors (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, etc.) are investing in provenance, watermarking, and metadata standards (e.g., C2PA / Content Credentials), but adoption is incomplete and not universal. That means provenance metadata will be very useful where present, but cannot be relied on for all content.

Newer research shows fingerprinting and watermarking techniques exist, but there are also methods (fine-tuning, paraphrasing, "eraser" methods) that can remove or reduce fingerprints — so attribution is probabilistic and evolving. Expect false positives/negatives and adversarial removal attempts.
